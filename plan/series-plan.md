## Hub — *Two things a machine would need to end us, and how close we are to both*

~1,500–2,000 words. Share image: two-axis chart with skull corner and anchors. First two sentences = preview text, state the claim.

1. **The gap.** AI on your phone vs AI inside the labs. News peg: OpenAI maths release (6 Oct), Royal Society letter (16 Sept), CASP intelligence-explosion report (Sept).
2. **The claim.** Two capabilities, together, before we can control them: a machine that invents new science on its own, and one that runs industry from mine to factory without people.
3. **The witnesses.** Pachocki, Hubinger, Christiano, Hinton, Bengio, Gowers, Scholze. Builders warning about what they build.
4. **Thing one: inventing on its own.** Maths breadth superhuman; hacking; AI writing most lab code, 26% of Anthropic R&D Claude-led; research taste near human expert. Swarm line: "a thousand copies organised themselves, and none of them told anyone."
5. **Thing two: factories without us.** Physics as the grader, simulators, humanoids (1X as Norwegian hook). The half almost nobody studies.
6. **Why both.** AI 2027 and IABIED share one order of events: we are kept alive while needed to run the factories.
7. **What still stands in the way.** One unexplained failure (dropping a frame that doesn't work), and swarms may get around it.
8. **Why a speed limit isn't enough.** Defence by anticipation fails exactly here; models know when they're tested. CASP and Klein ask for less than their own risk descriptions call for.
9. **The ask.** Immediate global halt on general-purpose frontier development; narrow tools continue. Own the change from the September essay and say why.
10. **What I don't know.**
